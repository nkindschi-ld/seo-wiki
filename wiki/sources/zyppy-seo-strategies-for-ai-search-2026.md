# These SEO Strategies Drive 90% of Your AI Visibility (Cyrus Shepard / Zyppy)

**Citation:** Shepard, Cyrus. "These SEO Strategies Drive 90% of Your AI
Visibility." Zyppy Signal (Substack). Published 2026-08-04.
https://signal.zyppy.com/p/seo-strategies-for-ai-search

**Rigor note:** a practitioner synthesis/opinion piece ("research-based
analysis... conducted over several months," no disclosed dataset or
methodology for the headline "90%" claim itself) organizing existing
mechanisms into a three-stage framework, plus a few genuinely new tool/
framework mentions. Treat the framing and headline stat as argued
opinion, the individual mechanism claims as consistent with (mostly
already-covered) sourced findings elsewhere in this wiki.

## Key takeaways

- **Core thesis**: most businesses can reach 90%+ of their AI-visibility
  goals via adapted classic-SEO fundamentals plus targeted tweaks for
  AI-specific mechanisms, rather than a wholesale separate "GEO"
  strategy — framed under the (Google-attributed, unsourced-in-article)
  line **"good SEO is good GEO."** This directly supports the
  "SEO fundamentals remain the foundation" side of
  [[generative-engine-optimization]]'s existing Conflicting Evidence
  entry (Google's own guidance vs. the founding GEO paper) as another
  practitioner voice — not a new conflict, an additional data point on
  an already-tracked question. The 90% figure itself is asserted, not
  measured.
- **Three-stage framework** (eligibility → citation → recommendation) —
  restates, rather than extends, this wiki's existing retrieval-
  eligibility / citation-stage / mention-vs-citation distinctions
  already covered across [[how-google-search-works]],
  [[ai-citation-landscape]], and [[generative-engine-optimization]].
- **New tool: AI Difference Engine** (browser extension, Gray Dot Co) —
  for diffing what an AI crawler vs. a human browser actually sees on a
  page (JS-rendering-gap diagnosis) — new to this wiki's technical-SEO
  tool list, complementary to the existing JS-rendering-gap findings in
  [[technical-seo-audit-checklist]] and
  [[vercel-rise-of-the-ai-crawler]].
- **New tool: AlsoAsked.com** — surfaces "People Also Ask"-style
  question data as a proxy for likely fan-out subqueries, giving a
  free/cheap alternative discovery method alongside the paid/API tools
  already logged in [[geo-content-optimization-tactics]]'s fan-out
  workflow section (from [[zyppy-fan-out-framework-2026]]).
- **A named brand-positioning formula**: "[Brand] is a [product] for
  [audience], especially [use case], because [differentiator]" — a
  one-sentence structure for consistent brand positioning across owned
  properties. Conceptually overlaps heavily with
  [[explainable-to-ai-4cs]]'s existing Category/Customer/Contrast/
  Consistency framework (Category≈product, Customer≈audience,
  Contrast≈differentiator, plus this formula's added "especially [use
  case]" specificity and Consistency as an explicit fourth requirement
  in both). Not a conflict — a shorter, single-sentence alternative
  phrasing of the same underlying positioning discipline, worth noting
  as a second independent voice converging on the same structure.
- **Distributed consensus for recommendations**: recommendations
  reportedly require multiple independent trusted sources agreeing, not
  just one strong page — consistent with, not new beyond, this wiki's
  existing third-party-authority and citation-vs-mention findings
  ([[ai-visibility-correlation-factors]], [[victorious-q2-2026-quarterly-search-report]]'s
  99.99% third-party-citation-dominance figure).
- **Publisher-pushback counterpoint (Shahzad Abbas, no link given)**:
  publishers increasingly want protections against *unlimited* AI bot
  access — a brief acknowledgment that more crawl access isn't
  universally the optimal publisher position, though the article
  doesn't develop this beyond a passing note. New framing (the
  publisher-interest angle) on an already-covered fact
  ([[robots-txt-strategy]] already notes AI crawlers don't always
  respect robots.txt).
