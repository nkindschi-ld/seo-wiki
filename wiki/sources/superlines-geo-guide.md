# The Complete 10-Step Guide to Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) in 2026 (Superlines, Jere Meriluoto)

**Citation:** Jere Meriluoto, "The Complete 10-Step Guide to Generative
Engine Optimization (GEO) in 2026," Superlines, published 2025-10-20,
updated 2026-04-16.
https://www.superlines.io/articles/generative-engine-optimization-geo-guide

**Vendor content marketing, undisclosed methodology throughout.** The
author's company (Superlines) sells GEO visibility-tracking software —
the KPI benchmarks, budget figures, and several statistics are presented
without sources or samples and should be read as a vendor's marketing
framing, not independent research. Several statistics are attributed
secondhand to third parties (Forrester, Adobe, a Kevin Indig LinkedIn
analysis) without a verifiable direct link — treat as unconfirmed
citations, not verified facts. Most of the 10-step framework and
technical/E-E-A-T guidance restates tactics already well-established in
this wiki.

## Key takeaways

- **Three-layer AI search model** (Training Data / High-Volume AI
  Search / Agentic AI), distinguished by retrieval speed and the
  marketer action each implies — a different, complementary lens to the
  wiki's existing static/search-augmented/reasoning-model LLM taxonomy
  in [[generative-engine-optimization]] (that taxonomy classifies LLM
  *products*; this one classifies *retrieval speed tiers* a given
  product might operate across).
- **"LLMs cite only 2-7 domains per response vs. Google's 10 blue
  links"** — an unsourced concentration claim, directionally consistent
  with this wiki's existing citation-concentration findings (e.g.
  [[ahrefs-b2b-seo-statistics-2025]]'s 28.9%-of-citations-to-top-50-brands
  figure) but not independently verified here.
- **A named GEO KPI matrix** (Citation Frequency, AI Brand Visibility,
  Share of Voice/AI SOV, Context Accuracy, Prompt Coverage, Assisted
  Conversions) with specific numeric starter benchmarks — useful as a
  naming/structure reference for what to measure, but the benchmark
  numbers themselves (e.g. ">95% context accuracy," "50-70% prompt
  coverage") have no disclosed basis and come from a vendor selling the
  measurement tool — treat as illustrative targets, not validated
  norms.
- **Recommends an `llms.txt` file** — another voice on the same
  unresolved scope question already flagged on
  [[geo-content-optimization-tactics]] (Google's guidance says it
  doesn't help its own AI Overviews/AI Mode; this and
  [[sel-ai-optimization-content-for-search-and-agents]] recommend it for
  the broader ecosystem without evidence either way).
- **Prompt-pack tracking template** (prompt pattern × example × intent ×
  cadence × owner) — an operational template for the same
  weekly-prompt-tracking practice already recommended elsewhere in this
  wiki (per [[similarweb-how-to-be-the-brand-ai-recommends-2026]]'s
  structured-prompt-set guidance) — not new in substance, but a
  concrete template worth noting.
- **Budget/team-structure guidance** (€50k-130k mid-market, €230k+
  enterprise; 4-role team) — vendor-proposed, no disclosed basis; not
  propagated into the wiki given the lack of any supporting data.
- **Secondhand market stats** (2.5B daily ChatGPT prompts, 71%/89%/87%
  adoption figures, "ChatGPT surpasses Google by Oct 2030") — not
  propagated into the wiki; each is attributed to a different
  third-party source without a verifiable direct citation, and this
  wiki already has more directly-sourced traffic-scale data via
  [[ai-traffic-scale-vs-hype]].
