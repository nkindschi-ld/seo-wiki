# 1 in 8 Social Media AI Citations Point to LinkedIn (Otterly.ai)

**Citation:** Rick Tousseyn. "1 in 8 Social Media AI Citations Point to
LinkedIn: AI Search Study by OtterlyAI." Otterly.ai Blog, 2026-06-03.
https://otterly.ai/blog/linkedin-ai-search-citations-study/

## What this source is

A large-scale, disclosed-methodology study of LinkedIn-specific AI
citation behavior: **2,061,071 citation records**, **384,205 unique
LinkedIn URLs**, **1,310,455 deduplicated citations**, across six
engines (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Perplexity,
Microsoft Copilot, Gemini), January-June 2026. Same author/methodology
family as [[otterly-url-ai-citations-study-2026]] (URL structure study,
ingested 2026-07-22), narrowed to one platform. Author identity was
resolved for 88.5% of URLs; gender was inferred from names/pronouns/
profile signals for a subset of that group.

## Key findings

- **LinkedIn's AI-citation share is growing fast**: ~1-in-8 social-media
  citations across the six engines point to LinkedIn, up from 7.8%
  (January 2026) to 11.7% (May 2026) — a 49.9% month-over-month growth
  rate in absolute citation volume.
- **Pulse articles (long-form) dominate over posts and profiles**:
  63.0% of URLs but 72.2% of citations, averaging 8.5 citations/URL —
  vs. posts (32.8% of URLs, 26.1% of citations, 5.9 avg) and profiles
  (4.1% of URLs, only 1.7% of citations, 3.0 avg). Pulse articles run
  far longer (median 1,021 words vs. 185 for posts).
- **Named individual authorship strongly outperforms company/brand
  pages**: named individuals account for 87.8% of URLs but 91.7% of
  citations (8.5 avg citations/URL) vs. 12.2%/8.3% for
  company/unattributed content (5.5 avg).
- **Engagement metrics show near-zero correlation with citation**:
  likes r = -0.06, comments r = -0.04, emojis r = -0.02, hashtags
  r = -0.02, word count alone r = 0.03. Media presence shows *no*
  citation advantage — posts with video actually averaged *fewer*
  citations than posts without (5.85 vs. 7.87); same pattern for images
  (7.14 vs. 8.23). The study's framing: "what gets a post liked does
  not get it cited."
- **Citations follow a steep power law on LinkedIn specifically**: top
  1% of URLs draw 30.2% of all citations, top 5% draw 54.6%, top 25%
  draw 83.3% — and **87.4% of LinkedIn URLs are cited only once** across
  all six engines combined.
- **Platform split is uneven**: Perplexity drives 43.3% of all LinkedIn
  citations (567,133), Google AI Overviews 22.2%, ChatGPT 18.7%, Google
  AI Mode 9.0%, Microsoft Copilot 6.8% (near-zero for Gemini, 38
  citations total). Copilot leans hardest on Pulse articles
  specifically (90.2% of its LinkedIn citations), vs. ~70-74% for the
  other major engines.
- **Gender disparity in citation**: among identifiable individual
  authors, men received 76.4% of citations vs. 23.5% for women,
  consistent (23-24% for women) across every major platform studied.
  **The study does not establish a cause** — this could reflect
  authorship demographics of the underlying LinkedIn content pool
  itself (e.g. who publishes Pulse articles/thought-leadership content
  on the platform) rather than a citation-selection bias specific to
  the AI engines. Reporting factually as a correlational finding with
  an unexamined cause, not as evidence of algorithmic bias one way or
  the other.

## Relationship to existing wiki claims

- **Precisely quantifies and strongly corroborates an existing, only
  vaguely-sourced claim**: [[growth-memo-topics-matter-for-third-party-authority]]
  already stated (via "LinkedIn's analysis, cited in the source" — a
  secondhand attribution with no disclosed sample size) that named,
  expert-authored content outperforms brand-account content, feeding
  the "named authors over brand bylines" tactic in
  [[geo-content-optimization-tactics]]. This study gives that claim a
  primary, disclosed-methodology, 384K-URL data point: 91.7% vs. 8.3%
  citation share, an 8.5-vs-5.5 average-citations gap.
- **Extends the citation power-law/concentration theme**: adds a
  platform-specific data point alongside
  [[otterly-url-ai-citations-study-2026]]'s general-web power law
  (15.8% of URLs → 50% of citations) and
  [[ai-citation-landscape]]'s existing concentration findings — LinkedIn
  shows an even steeper concentration (top 1% → 30.2%) with the same
  long-tail shape.
- **Extends the engine sourcing-"personality" framing**: adds LinkedIn-
  specific platform reliance to
  [[brightedge-ai-search-same-brands-different-sources]]'s
  engine-personality profiles — Perplexity draws disproportionately
  more from LinkedIn than the other engines (43.3% of all LinkedIn
  citations captured by Perplexity alone), and Copilot's LinkedIn
  citations are almost entirely long-form Pulse articles (90.2%).
- **New to this wiki**: no existing source measured engagement metrics
  (likes, comments, media presence) against AI citation likelihood.
  This is a first, disclosed-methodology null result on that question —
  consistent in spirit with [[ai-visibility-correlation-factors]]'s
  existing "content volume shows almost no correlation" finding
  (surface/vanity signals don't predict AI citation), but a distinct
  metric (engagement rather than volume).
