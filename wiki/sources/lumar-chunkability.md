# Chunkability: Why Some Content Gets Cited, and Most Doesn't (Lumar)

Lumar Editorial Team, Lumar blog, published 2026-08-24 (modified
2026-09-09). Part 4 of Lumar's GEO series, after
[[lumar-semantic-relevance-three-jobs]].

**Rigor:** practitioner framework with no data or tests ("in our
experience"). The four pillars are an assessment rubric, not measured
factors.

## Key takeaways

1. **Definition:** chunkability is how well a section works when it's
   separated from the rest of the page. A highly chunkable passage is
   *atomic*: one complete idea or answer inside a clear section. It is **not**
   word count, a readability score, formatting alone, or splitting long pages
   into smaller ones.
2. **Where it matters:** at the passage level, for how precisely a chunk is
   retrieved, whether it is chosen for citation, and how confident the answer
   is. It is why a long, relevant page can go uncited while a short, precise
   passage gets used.
3. **Four pillars:**
   - *Topical cohesion:* one topic or answer per chunk, with no drift.
   - *Boundary clarity:* the chunk opens with a clear premise and ends when
     the idea is done, so concepts don't get split across chunks.
   - *Answer density:* entities, units, constraints and steps, without
     padding.
   - *Self-contained meaning:* no "as mentioned above" and no pronouns
     without a clear referent.
4. **Low chunkability isn't bad writing.** Common patterns: long intros,
   several FAQ questions in one section, steps split across headings, and
   tables that depend on text elsewhere.
5. **Targeted fixes, not rewrites:** split multi-topic sections, move key
   facts into the passage that needs them, build each section around one
   question, and replace ambiguous pronouns with the entities they refer to.
