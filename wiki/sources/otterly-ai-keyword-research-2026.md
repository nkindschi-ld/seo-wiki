# AI Keyword Research in 2026 (Otterly.ai)

**Citation:** Thomas Peham. "AI Keyword Research in 2026: How to Win
Citations in ChatGPT, Perplexity & Google AI Overviews." Otterly.ai
Blog, 2026-04-01. https://otterly.ai/blog/ai-keyword-research/

## What this source is

A tactics/opinion guide on AI-era keyword research, same author as
[[otterly-how-to-optimize-content-for-ai-search-2026]] (published
2026-02-16, ingested earlier the same day as this source). **Heavy
overlap with that prior ingest**: identical query-volume figures
(Google AI Overviews ~15B/day, ChatGPT ~2.5B/day), the same FAQ-schema
350% citation-lift experiment (2,379 vs. 529), the same citation-type
breakdown (ChatGPT 44.7% brand/25.1% news/8.1% forum/6.3% encyclopedia;
Google AI Overviews 59.8% brand), the same "95% of citations from
third-party sites" claim, and the same "ChatGPT drives 56% of AI
referral traffic" figure — none re-filed here, see the prior source
page. Its seven-category keyword taxonomy, content-structure
checklist, and six-metric measurement framework restate tactics
already covered in [[geo-content-optimization-tactics]] (query-fanout
targeting, answer-first structure, entity naming,
presence/portability/concentration-style measurement) without adding
new information.

## What's new

- **Real vs. estimated prompt characteristics** (OtterlyAI's own prompt
  research, 2025): real user prompts average **15.1 words vs. 8.8**
  for keyword-tool-estimated prompts, personal pronouns appear in
  **52%** of real prompts, and **78.9%** of real prompts show
  tool-finding intent vs. **62.5%** of estimated prompts. A genuinely
  new methodological finding — actual AI-search queries run longer,
  more personal, and more action-oriented than what keyword-research
  tools typically surface, with a direct implication for how to build
  a keyword list for AI-search targeting (mine real query logs/support
  tickets/community language rather than relying on estimated-volume
  keyword tools alone).
- **2025 OtterlyAI experiment: `llms.txt`, author schema, and YouTube
  content all showed no measurable citation lift**, while Wikipedia
  presence, LinkedIn Pulse posts, FAQ-on-homepage, and digital PR did.
  - The `llms.txt` null result is a useful addition to
    [[geo-content-optimization-tactics]]'s existing guidance: Google
    already says `llms.txt` doesn't help its own AI features, and
    [[sel-ai-optimization-content-for-search-and-agents]] recommends it
    for the broader AI-crawler ecosystem with no evidence it helps
    there either. This experiment, if it tested citation lift across
    multiple engines (not disclosed which), is the first source in
    this wiki to report a **direct negative test** of `llms.txt`
    specifically, rather than just an absence of a positive claim.
  - **Author schema: no lift** — new to this wiki, no prior claim to
    compare against.
  - **YouTube content: no lift** — this is a genuine **nuance, not a
    contradiction**, against [[ai-visibility-correlation-factors]]'s
    existing finding that YouTube *mentions* correlate strongest
    (~0.737) of all measured factors with AI-mention visibility. These
    are plausibly different mechanisms: Ahrefs measured whether a brand
    is *discussed/mentioned* on YouTube (a brand-level correlational
    signal, aggregated across the whole platform), while this
    experiment tested whether *publishing your own YouTube content*
    directly lifts citation rate for that content (a page-level
    causal-adjacent test). A brand could benefit from being talked
    about on YouTube broadly while a single company-published video
    still fails to get directly cited — not necessarily incompatible,
    but worth flagging since it points in different directions on
    "should I invest in YouTube."

## Not filed — low rigor, marginal value

- "15% of total website traffic now arrives via AI agents and bots" —
  no methodology given, and the definition is ambiguous (conflates AI
  crawler/bot traffic with AI-referral human traffic, two very
  different things already tracked separately elsewhere in this wiki).
- Gemini (18%) and Perplexity (8%) shares of AI referral traffic — more
  granular than, but no more sourced than, the already-flagged-as-
  unverified 56% ChatGPT figure from the prior Otterly source. Not
  worth compounding an unsourced figure with more unsourced figures.
