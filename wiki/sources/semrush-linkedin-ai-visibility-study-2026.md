# We Analyzed 89K LinkedIn URLs Cited in AI Search (Semrush)

**Citation:** Margarita Loktionova, "We Analyzed 89K LinkedIn URLs Cited in AI
Search: Here's What Drives Visibility," Semrush Blog, 2026-03-10.
https://www.semrush.com/blog/linkedin-ai-visibility-study/

A disclosed-methodology study of **89,000 unique LinkedIn URLs** cited across
**325,000 prompts** on **three engines** (ChatGPT Search, Google AI Mode,
Perplexity), 12 industries, Jan–Feb 2026. The **second** LinkedIn-specific
AI-citation study in the wiki and a strong independent cross-check on
[[otterly-linkedin-ai-citations-study-2026]] (Otterly, 384K URLs, 6 engines,
Jan–Jun 2026) — which it largely corroborates, with one real divergence on
Perplexity's company-vs-individual mix.

## Key findings

- **LinkedIn ≈ #2 source, ~11% of AI responses** cite it on average
  (ChatGPT 14.3%, Google AI Mode 13.5%, Perplexity 5.3% — *% of responses
  citing LinkedIn*).
- **Original long-form dominates**: 500–2,000-word articles + 50–299-word posts
  cited most; **95% of citations are original content**; reshares only 5%.
  Articles are 50–66% of citations, feed posts 15–28%.
- **Educational content wins**: 54–64% of cited posts share knowledge/practical
  advice (Google AI Mode ~66%).
- **Consistency beats virality**: median cited post has 15–25 reactions, <1
  comment; **75% of cited authors post 5+ times / four weeks**.
- **Vanity signals don't drive citation**: ~50% of cited authors have 2,000+
  followers, but authors with <500 followers are cited **equally or more** than
  those with 500+.
- **Semantic similarity 0.57–0.60** — LinkedIn content mirrors AI-answer meaning
  more closely than Reddit (0.53–0.54) or Quora (0.435).
- **Company vs. individual splits by engine**: Perplexity **59% Company Pages**;
  ChatGPT Search **59% individual creators**; Google AI Mode **59% individual
  creators**.

## Relationship to existing wiki claims

**Strongly corroborates [[otterly-linkedin-ai-citations-study-2026]]** on four
independent axes (different sample, engine set, and time window):
- **~11% LinkedIn citation share** — both studies land at ~11%.
- **Long-form articles dominate over short posts** — Semrush articles 50–66% of
  citations; Otterly Pulse articles 72.2%.
- **Engagement doesn't predict citation** — Semrush "consistency beats
  virality," median 15–25 reactions; Otterly near-zero engagement correlations
  (r = -0.02 to -0.06), "what gets liked doesn't get cited."
- **Follower/vanity count is not the driver** — Semrush's <500-follower authors
  cited equally/more; consistent with Otterly's engagement null and
  [[ai-visibility-correlation-factors]]'s brand-size null on LinkedIn.

**Adds genuinely new tactics** not in the Otterly study:
- **Posting consistency/frequency** as a citation correlate (75% of cited
  authors post 5+/four weeks) — a cadence signal distinct from any single
  post's quality.
- **Originality (95%) and educational intent (54–64%)** quantified as
  citation-dominant, and explicit **word-count targets** (articles 500–2,000;
  posts 50–299).
- **Semantic-similarity ranking** of LinkedIn vs. Reddit vs. Quora.

**One real divergence — Perplexity company-vs-individual (logged as Conflicting
Evidence on [[ai-citation-landscape]]):** Otterly found named individuals draw
**91.7%** of LinkedIn citations aggregate across 6 engines; Semrush found
**Perplexity specifically cites 59% Company Pages** (while ChatGPT and Google AI
Mode are 59% individual — those two agree with Otterly). Because Otterly also
found **Perplexity drives the single largest share of LinkedIn citations
(43.3%)**, a strong Perplexity company-page tilt is hard to fully reconcile with
a 91.7%-individual aggregate. Likely explanations: differing definitions
("Company Pages" vs. Otterly's "company/unattributed"; a Company-Page-published
*post* vs. an individual's Pulse article), different engine sets (3 vs. 6), and
non-overlapping time windows (Jan–Feb vs. Jan–Jun). Both agree individuals
dominate on ChatGPT and Google AI Mode.

**Note — platform rates aren't directly comparable across the two studies.**
Semrush's "Perplexity 5.3%" is *% of responses that cite LinkedIn*; Otterly's
"Perplexity 43.3%" is *share of all LinkedIn citations captured by that engine*.
Perplexity can cite LinkedIn in a smaller fraction of responses yet still
account for the largest slice of total LinkedIn citation volume (more
citations/fanout per response). Not a conflict — different denominators.

## Assessment

Solid, disclosed methodology from a credible vendor with a commercial tie-in
(AI Visibility Toolkit) — treat the tool mention as marketing, but the study
design (89K URLs, 325K prompts, per-platform breakdowns) is sound and its four
major findings replicate an independent study. Medium-high rigor; strongest as
the independent corroboration of the Otterly LinkedIn findings.
