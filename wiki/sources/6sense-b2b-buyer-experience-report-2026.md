# 2026 B2B Buyer Experience Report (6sense)

**Citation:** 6sense, with MarketOne, LeadFabric and Green Hat. "2026 B2B
Buyer Experience Report: The Secret Life of B2B Buyers."
https://6sense.com/buyer-experience-report-2026. No author named on the
page. Released 2026-11-02 (date taken from Demand Gen Report and MarTech
Edge coverage, not from the page itself). The press release gives 4,611
buyers; the appendix totals 3,596 + 1,011 = 4,607 journeys.

**Method:** three surveys of B2B buyers, each describing one recent
buying journey of $25K or more. 3,596 completed purchases and 1,011
stalled or abandoned evaluations. Respondents are mostly senior (34–43%
C-level) across North America, Europe, the UK and APAC. The mix is
services 44–52%, software 23–30% and physical goods 25–28%. Tests use
Bonferroni/Holm corrections and the report says plainly that results are
associations, not causes.

## Rigor and bias

- **Strong:** large sample, a separate stalled/abandoned survey (rare),
  year-over-year comparison with the 2025 edition, multiple-comparison
  corrections and stated limitations.
- **Weak:** self-reported recall of a past purchase; the survey panel
  isn't named; per-table sample sizes aren't given; small rounding
  mismatches between the summary and body (82% vs. 81.5%, 92% vs.
  91.7%); one table mislabels 6.8 evaluations as "6.8%".
- **Conflict of interest:** 6sense sells intent data and account-based
  marketing. The report's headline that LLMs are "a tweak, not a
  revolution" supports its own product, and it says some in the industry
  have questioned intent data because of the LLM-discovery story. The
  numbers can still be right, but weigh the framing with that in mind.
- **Scope:** committee B2B purchases of $25K or more, with software
  under a third of the sample. Don't apply it to consumer or
  self-serve/PLG purchases.

## Key takeaways

- **The shortlist is set before sales contact.** Buyers first contact
  sellers 59.2% of the way through a 10.3-month cycle. By then 91.4% have
  ranked a shortlist, 84.8% have set requirements, and the eventual
  winner was on the Day 1 shortlist in 91.7% of journeys. The pre-contact
  favorite wins 81.5% of the time.
- **LLMs are widely used but rarely decisive.** 81% use them (down from
  94% in 2025). Only 26% use them to find new vendors; most use them to
  sort, summarize and validate, and mostly mid-journey (59%) rather than
  at the start (43%). As a vendor-preference influence, AI tools score
  21.2% in Selection and 17.1% in Validation, below existing knowledge,
  internal discussion, analysts and review sites. 25.2% say LLMs
  "significantly" influenced their view of vendors.
- **LLMs rank last as a way to stay current.** Between cycles: vendor
  websites 35.9%, events 33.4%, review sites 31.4%, trade media 31.3% ...
  LLMs 15.0% (last of 11). 96% stay current between cycles.
- **Trust follows prompting effort.** 51.8% have high trust in LLM
  output. Prompting effort correlates with trust (r = .58) and with how
  much influence buyers attribute to LLMs (r = .47). The report asks
  whether buyers use LLMs "to research, or to confirm".
- **Forms undercount buyers.** Only 35.2% use their own contact details
  on a form. 31.6% have a teammate fill it, 19.3% use a personal email,
  17.7% use AI tools to avoid the form, and 7.4% skip gated content. The
  buyer's team starts 82% of first contacts. The report recommends
  making materials "findable, ungated, shareable".
- **Buyers know the category already.** ~70% have been through six or
  more cycles in the category, 85%+ of purchases are in Established or
  Maturing categories, 94% start with views on vendors already, and in
  71% of journeys the buying group starts with two or more vendor
  preferences.
- **Justifying the category is a separate, often harder sale.** 56.4% say
  agreeing on the category was as hard as or harder than choosing the
  vendor (64.4% in failed journeys, 61.5% for new capabilities). The
  report recommends two separate persuasion tracks: one for the category,
  one for the vendor.
- **Buying groups hold steady at ~10 people.** IT is in 69.9% of
  software purchases and finance is the most common supporting function.
  The report says B2B websites must serve purchasing, IT and finance, not
  only the main buyer persona.
- **Third parties shape decisions.** 74% use an outside resource; among
  purchasers, consultants (38.4%) and analysts (35.0%) lead.
- **Most "no decision" outcomes are paused, not dead.** 77.9% expect to
  revisit within 12 months; only 5% would approach it the same way.
- **Embedded AI is a common buying requirement.** It was part of 80% of
  purchases, and mandated or strongly encouraged in 58%. Buyer confidence
  that they understand how vendors actually use AI fell from 82.5% to
  72.7%, which the report calls a gap "providers can close with clearer
  content".
