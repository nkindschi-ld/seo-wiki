# SEO Case Study: How We Influenced the ChatGPT Search Results (Go Fish Digital, Chris Long)

**Citation:** Chris Long, "SEO Case Study: How We Influenced the ChatGPT
Search Results," Go Fish Digital Blog, 2025-07-25.
https://gofishdigital.com/blog/seo-case-study-how-we-influenced-the-chatgpt-search-results/

**n=1 anecdote**, single company, single query, no controlled comparison
or disclosed measurement methodology beyond "we changed the page and it
showed up about a week later" — treat as a concrete illustrative example
of an already-established mechanism, not new independent evidence.

## Key takeaways

- **Case study**: Go Fish Digital noticed competitors' ChatGPT Search
  listings for "digital PR agency" included a "Notable Clients" section
  that their own listing lacked. They identified an already-cited
  listicle article, added a "Notable Clients" bullet list structured as
  key-value pairs, and within ~1 week ChatGPT Search began surfacing
  notable clients in their own listing.
- **Reinforces, doesn't add new evidence for**, the existing wiki finding
  that ChatGPT Search retrieves via Bing's index (per
  [[semrush-chatgpt-search]]) — the tactic here (edit a page ChatGPT
  already cites, wait for propagation) is a live, concrete illustration
  of [[geo-content-optimization-tactics]]'s existing "build internal
  links from already-cited pages" tactic, extended to "edit already-cited
  pages directly," which wasn't explicitly stated before.
- **Key-value-pair bullet-list structuring** as a format specifically
  chosen to appeal to AI extraction — consistent with, and a concrete
  example of, existing AI-reuse formatting guidance (atomic paragraphs,
  predictable templates) already in [[geo-content-optimization-tactics]].
- **~1 week propagation time** for a content edit to surface in ChatGPT
  Search output — a first data point on GEO edit-to-visibility latency,
  not previously quantified anywhere in this wiki (though from a single
  case, not a measured average).
