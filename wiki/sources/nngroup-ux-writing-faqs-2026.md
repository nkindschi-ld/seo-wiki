# NN/g — UX Writing: FAQs from Practitioners (Kaley, 2026)

**Citation:** Anna Kaley, "UX Writing: FAQs from Practitioners," Nielsen Norman
Group, May 8, 2026. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-writing-faqs/

A themed Q&A compilation from NN/g's "Writing Compelling Digital Copy" course —
primarily a **UX-writing craft** article (plain language, voice/tone, jargon,
link/CTA labels, stakeholder buy-in). Ingested for its **narrow AEO/GEO-relevant
slice**, which mostly **corroborates** tactics already in the wiki rather than adding
new evidence. No methodology/study of its own on the AI-search claims — this is
expert practitioner guidance, not a measured study.

## Why it's in this wiki (the in-scope slice)

- **"Write for people first. Structure it for machines second."** Directly restates
  the wiki's existing people-first framing (see [[google-ai-optimization-guide]] and
  the "write for people first" note in [[geo-content-optimization-tactics]]). SEO,
  AEO, and GEO are presented as complementary, with underlying principles *unchanged*:
  "clear structure, plain language, descriptive headings, **direct answers near the
  top**" — i.e. the answer-first pattern the wiki already treats as core AEO guidance.
- **Traffic → engagement measurement shift.** As AI tools intercept searches before
  the click, the article says to judge content success by **engagement, conversion,
  task-completion, and return visits** rather than raw traffic. Added as a light
  supporting note to [[aio-ctr-impact]]'s measurement-lesson section (a qualitative,
  KPI-philosophy corroboration of that page's quantitative zero-click findings).
- **Scannability data.** NN/g eyetracking: users read only **20–28% of page text**;
  digital readers "scan, enter pages mid-way, jump between sections" (hence: spell out
  acronyms repeatedly, use literal/descriptive headings and link text, chunk content).
  Corroborates the wiki's extractability/atomic-structure guidance.
- **AI as "a capable first-draft collaborator"** — useful for drafts/headlines/alt
  text/brainstorming, but risks generic output and brand-voice drift; always review.

## Notable nuance / mild tension (not logged as a conflict)

- **Reading level.** NN/g recommends a **6th–8th grade** reading level *for human
  comprehension/scanning* of general-audience content. This is **not** the same
  outcome as [[airops-fan-out-effect-2026]]'s finding that a **college reading level
  (Flesch-Kincaid 16–17)** correlates with higher *AI citation*. Different dependent
  variables (human task success vs. machine citation), different audiences — noted
  here so the two aren't mistaken for a contradiction. Practical read: write simply
  for human readers; don't dumb content down purely chasing an AI-citation reading-
  level number, and don't assume either figure transfers to the other's goal.
