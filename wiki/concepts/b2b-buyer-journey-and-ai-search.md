# The B2B Buyer Journey and AI Search

How B2B buying groups research, shortlist and choose vendors, and how
much of that search and AI visibility actually reaches. This matters for
SEO/AEO planning because in committee B2B buying, the winner is usually
decided before a buyer ever fills in a form or talks to sales. The
visibility that counts is what a buyer's group has absorbed over years,
not one search session.

Main source: [[6sense-b2b-buyer-experience-report-2026]] (4,607 buying
journeys of $25K or more, released 2026-11-02). Earlier editions'
figures appear second-hand in [[ahrefs-b2b-seo-statistics-2025]].
Scope: committee purchases, mostly services and physical goods (software
is under a third of the sample). It does not describe consumer or
self-serve/PLG buying.

## Buying is deliberation, not discovery

- **Buyers know the category already.** About 70% of buyers have been
  through six or more buying cycles in the category. 85%+ of purchases
  are in Established or Maturing categories. Even "new capability"
  purchases are mostly in categories buyers call Established (48.8%) or
  Maturing (34.9%).
- **Opinions exist on Day 1.** 94% start with views on vendors already.
  In 71% of journeys the buying group (~10 people) starts with two or
  more vendor preferences; only 4.2% start with none.
- **Buyers keep watching between cycles.** 96% stay current between
  purchases, and 71.5% say their view of vendors changed in that time.
  Those changed views become the next cycle's Day 1 shortlist.
- **Buyers pick vendors they know.** In 73% of journeys the buyer (or
  their organization) had already bought or used the eventual winner.

What it means: content and visibility build preference over years
(brand, docs, reviews, analyst and peer presence), not just capture at
the moment of a search. This fits [[ai-traffic-scale-vs-hype]]'s point
that "influence precedes search."

## The shortlist is set before sales contact

| Milestone | Share |
|---|---|
| Winner already on the Day 1 shortlist | 91.7% |
| Shortlist ranked before first seller contact | 91.4% |
| Requirements largely or entirely set before contact | 84.8% |
| Pre-contact favorite ultimately wins | 81.5% |
| Eventual winner was the first vendor contacted | 76.6% |
| Buyer's team (not the vendor) starts first contact | 82.2% |

First contact happens 59.2% of the way through a 10.3-month cycle (63%
in North America). These numbers barely vary by buying role or region.
Getting on the shortlist is the main goal for SEO and AEO in B2B, and
the work is mostly unseen because it happens before anyone identifies
themselves. The Ahrefs compilation of earlier editions (81% have a
preferred vendor before outreach, 71% pick their first shortlisted
option) agrees.

## Where AI fits: widely used, a secondary influence

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Buyers using LLMs anywhere in the journey | 81% (94% in 2025) |
| Use LLMs to **find new vendors** | 26% |
| Use LLMs mid-journey / at the start (multi-select) | 59% / 43% |
| LLMs "significantly" influenced view of vendors | 25.2% |
| AI tools as a vendor-preference influence, Selection / Validation | 21.2% / 17.1% |
| LLMs as a channel for staying current between cycles | 15.0% (last of 11) |
| High / moderate trust in LLM output | 51.8% / 36.5% |

Buyers mostly use LLMs to **sort, summarize and check** what they
already know, not to find vendors they've never heard of. Prompting
effort correlates with trust (r = .58) and with how much influence
buyers attribute to LLMs (r = .47). The report asks whether buyers use
LLMs "to research, or to confirm". If it's to confirm, the job for AI
visibility in B2B is to make sure LLMs describe a brand accurately and
favorably once a buyer already has it in mind, more than getting
discovered.

**Influence ranking in the Selection phase:** existing knowledge 34.4%,
internal discussion 31.8%, analysts/consultants 24.5%, review sites
23.8%, AI tools 21.2%, vendor web content 21.2%. Every influence except
live sales conversations drops in Validation.

**Channels for staying current between cycles:** vendor websites 35.9%,
events 33.4%, review sites 31.4%, trade media 31.3%, informal seller
conversations 24.1%, social 23.9%, peers 23.8%, analysts 22.9%, podcasts
19.7%, vendor communities 18.7%, LLMs 15.0%.

Caveat: 6sense sells intent data, and the "LLMs are a tweak, not a
revolution" framing helps its own product. See the rigor notes on
[[6sense-b2b-buyer-experience-report-2026]].

## What a B2B site has to do

- **Serve the whole buying group.** IT is in 69.9% of software
  purchases, finance is the most common supporting function, and
  procurement is in 38.1% of software purchases. The report says
  websites must serve "the purchasing, IT, and finance representatives,"
  not only the main persona. Security, pricing, integration and ROI
  pages are buying-group content, not just sales-support content.
- **Make the case for the category as well as the vendor.** 56.4% say
  agreeing on the category was as hard as choosing a vendor, or harder.
  That rises to 61.5% for new-capability purchases and 64.4% in failed
  journeys. The report recommends two separate persuasion tracks. For
  the category: quantified outcomes, reasons to change, confidence in
  implementation, and material finance can reuse. For the vendor: fit
  and proof. Category-level explainer and glossary content
  ([[glossary-pages-for-seo]]) serves the first.
- **Don't count on gates or forms to capture buyers.** Only 35.2% use
  their real contact details on a form. 31.6% have a teammate fill it,
  19.3% use a personal email, 17.7% use AI to avoid the form, and 7.4%
  skip gated content. The report recommends materials that are
  "findable, ungated, shareable." Ungated content is also what crawlers
  and LLMs can read (see [[lead-magnet-checklist]]).
- **Be clear about embedded AI.** AI was part of 80% of purchases and
  mandated or strongly encouraged in 58%. Buyer confidence that they
  understand how vendors actually use AI fell from 82.5% to 72.7%, a gap
  the report says clearer content can close.
- **Cut the hype.** The report expects buyer patience with "unhelpful
  hyperbole and marketing speak" to keep falling now that LLMs
  summarize vendor sites for them.
- **Work the third parties.** Among purchasers, consultants (38.4%) and
  analysts (35.0%) are the most-used outside resources. Review sites are
  both a top influence and a top channel for staying current. This
  overlaps with what AI engines cite (see [[ai-citation-landscape]] and
  [[listicles-in-ai-search]]).

## Paused deals come back

Stalled evaluations still ran 8.6 months and engaged 3.3 vendors. 77.9%
expect to revisit within 12 months and only 5% would approach it the
same way (budget, vendors, stakeholders and requirements are expected to
change). For content, that means a returning buyer is better informed
and has new objections. It also argues for keeping category and
comparison content current rather than treating it as finished.

## Conflicting Evidence

- **Claim**: buyers increasingly make purchase decisions inside AI chat,
  so AI share of voice works like retail shelf space.
  - Supported by: [[tryprofound-shortlist-is-the-new-shelf]]
    (2026-07-14). 92.8% of ChatGPT purchase tasks ended with no web
    click, and visibility correlated 0.57 with selection. That was 56
    consumer participants (grocery, insurance, apps).
  - Contradicted by: [[6sense-b2b-buyer-experience-report-2026]]
    (2026-11-02). In committee B2B buying of $25K or more, only 26% use
    LLMs to find vendors, LLMs rank last as a way to stay current, and
    their use fell from 94% to 81%.
- **Current best guess**: the two mostly don't overlap; they describe
  different kinds of purchase. In-chat shortlisting looks strong for
  individual, low-familiarity consumer choices. In committee B2B buying
  in familiar categories, AI is a secondary influence used to confirm a
  choice. Both studies have biases: Profound sells AI-visibility
  tracking and 6sense sells intent data, and each result supports its
  author's product. Unresolved. A B2B-specific behavioral study (not a
  survey) would settle it. Logged as a `conflict` on 2026-11-19.
