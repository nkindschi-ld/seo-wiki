# Explainable to AI: The 4 C's

**Why / when to use this:** use when AI engines describe your brand vaguely, hedge, or
recommend competitors even though your product is well-regarded — i.e. when the problem
is **legibility, not likeability**. This is a *positioning/messaging* framework for
becoming recommendable, complementary to [[ai-entity-footprint-audit]] (the diagnostic:
*does* AI understand you?) and [[brand-entity-seo-strategy]] (the entity-signal build).
Based on [[derivatex-4cs-explainable-to-ai-2026]] (DerivateX / Apoorv Sharma), an
original tactical framework with attached first-party research.

**Core thesis:** "AI recommends the companies it can explain confidently." The winners
of AI search "are not the best-liked. They are the most legible." Corroborated by this
wiki's recognition-vs-mention data — [[victorious-q2-2026-quarterly-search-report]]
found 96% of brands described accurately but 89% never mentioned in category answers,
and DerivateX's own benchmark (below) found ~half of 50 B2B SaaS firms scored <50/100
on AI presence *despite* near-perfect favorability. Being liked is not being surfaced.

## The four C's

1. **Category** — the single, buyer-searchable bucket you occupy. Use the plain
   category buyers actually type ("real estate CRM," "construction management
   platform," "attendance software for deskless teams"), not invented/premium framings
   ("growth platform," "all-in-one solution"). An engine can only match you to a
   category it can name.
2. **Customer** — one narrow, identifiable segment, not everyone. "Lean IT teams at
   50–500-person companies" beats "for teams of every size." The failure mode:
   "vagueness matches nothing, so it gets matched to nothing" — narrower is *more*
   recommendable, not less. Test your stated customer against real buyer prompts
   ("attendance software for a small manufacturing team").
3. **Contrast** — one clear, repeatable reason to choose you that the model can restate
   *without hedging and without you present*. "The only attendance tool that works
   offline on a construction site" beats an adjective list ("powerful, flexible,
   secure, easy"). "Five differentiators do not make you five times more compelling to
   a machine. They blur."
4. **Consistency** — the same Category/Customer/Contrast language across every surface
   the model reads (homepage, G2, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, press). When signals conflict,
   "the model invents a version of you." This is the recommendation-wording counterpart
   to [[brand-entity-seo-strategy]]'s entity-line standardization and the "Consistency"
   dimension of [[ai-entity-footprint-audit]], and the same discipline the
   [[competitor-citation-displacement]] playbook applies to claims a rival owns.
   **Independently-arrived-at shorter version (2026-08-20)**: per
   [[zyppy-seo-strategies-for-ai-search-2026]], a single-sentence template — "[Brand]
   is a [product] for [audience], especially [use case], because [differentiator]" —
   covers the same Category/Customer/Contrast ground; see
   [[geo-content-optimization-tactics]] for the full note.

## The two-minute self-test

1. Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude in separate tabs.
2. Ask each: **"What does [company] do, who is it for, and why choose it?"**
3. Score each engine 0–2 per C (total out of 8).
4. Log every hedge, contradiction, or invented detail — those are your build targets.

**Scoring:** 7–8 = recommendable · 4–6 = inconsistent · 0–3 = effectively invisible.
Complements the platform-probe step in [[ai-entity-footprint-audit]] (a fuller
six-dimension, 0–5 diagnostic); the 4 C's self-test is the fast, positioning-specific
version.

## Attached research (first-party, small samples)

- **2026 State of AI Visibility benchmark:** 50 B2B SaaS companies, 1,400 buyer
  prompts, 4 engines — nearly half scored <50/100 on AI presence despite 44/50 scoring
  19–20/20 on favorability. "Well-liked but illegible" is the common failure.
- **Authority Inversion study:** 233 ChatGPT recommendations, 40 categories — 84% of
  citations came from vendor self-descriptions or niche sites; G2 and Capterra cited
  zero times; contradictory descriptions were "actively harmful." **This is in tension
  with the wiki's third-party-authority/review-platform guidance** — logged as
  unresolved Conflicting Evidence in [[ai-citation-landscape]]. Read the two together:
  the 4 C's improve the *recommendation wording* the engine builds from your own
  legible pages; third-party footprint (Victorious, review-platform presence) still
  drives whether you're *surfaced/mentioned* at all. Do both.

## Caveats

- **Vendor framework with small first-party samples** (n=50 companies; n=233
  recommendations, ChatGPT-only). The 4 C's are sound as positioning discipline; treat
  the specific company scores (Clio 89, ServiceTitan 68 vs. Jobber 41) as illustrative.
- **Legibility ≠ narrowing your actual product** — the tactic is about *compressible
  description*, not shrinking what you sell.

## Checklist

- [ ] Category: one plain, buyer-searchable bucket stated identically everywhere.
- [ ] Customer: one narrow, named segment; tested against real buyer prompts.
- [ ] Contrast: a single differentiator the model restates without hedging.
- [ ] Consistency: Category/Customer/Contrast aligned across homepage, G2, LinkedIn,
      Crunchbase, press.
- [ ] Ran the 4-engine self-test and logged hedges/contradictions/invented details.
- [ ] Paired legibility work with third-party/mention-side presence (not either/or).
