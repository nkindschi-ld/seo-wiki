## Core thesis

"AI recommends the companies it can explain confidently." Winning AI recommendation is
about **legibility, not likeability**: "The companies that win AI search are not the
best-liked. They are the most legible." "Being easy to explain is not dumbing down. It
is being compressible."

## The 4 C's

1. **Category** — the single, buyer-searchable bucket you occupy. Use the plain
   category buyers actually search ("real estate CRM," "construction management
   platform," "attendance software for deskless teams"); avoid invented/premium
   framings ("growth platform," "all-in-one solution"). Evidence cited: Clio (89/100)
   and Figma praised for "total category clarity"; REsimpli became ChatGPT's top
   recommendation within 90 days after adopting "real estate CRM" positioning.
2. **Customer** — the specific segment you serve, not everyone. Name a narrow,
   identifiable buyer group ("lean IT teams at 50–500-person companies" beats "for
   teams of every size"). Narrower is "more recommendable, not less"; vagueness
   "matches nothing, so it gets matched to nothing."
3. **Contrast** — one clear, repeatable reason to choose you. A single differentiator
   the model can restate without hedging ("the only attendance tool that works offline
   on a construction site") beats adjective lists ("powerful, flexible, secure, easy").
   Test: can the model repeat it accurately without you present? Evidence: ServiceTitan
   68 vs. Jobber 41 in field service (27-pt gap attributed to clarity, not product
   quality). "Five differentiators do not make you five times more compelling to a
   machine. They blur."
4. **Consistency** — agreement across every source the model reads (homepage, G2,
   LinkedIn, Crunchbase, press). When signals conflict, the model "often invents a
   version of you instead." Evidence: the top-10 companies held #1 across all four
   models simultaneously via "cross-platform agreement."

## Two-minute self-test

1. Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude in separate tabs.
2. Ask each: "What does [company] do, who is it for, and why choose it?"
3. Score 0–2 per C (total /8).
4. Note hedging, contradictions, invented details.
Scoring: 7–8 recommendable · 4–6 inconsistent · 0–3 effectively invisible.

## Original research data attached

- **2026 State of AI Visibility benchmark:** 50 B2B SaaS companies, 1,400 buyer prompts
  across ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini/Claude. Nearly half scored below 50/100 on AI
  presence **despite** positive sentiment (44/50 scored 19–20/20 favorability) — i.e.
  well-liked but illegible.
- **Authority Inversion study:** 233 ChatGPT recommendations across 40 software
  categories; **84% of citations came from vendor self-descriptions or niche sites;
  G2 and Capterra were cited zero times.** Models built answers from single clear
  pages, making contradictory descriptions actively harmful.
