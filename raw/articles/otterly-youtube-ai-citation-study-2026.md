# The YouTube Citation Study 2026

**Author:** Rick Tousseyn
**Publish date:** 2026-03-02
**Source:** Otterly.ai Blog
**URL:** https://otterly.ai/blog/youtube-ai-citation-study-2026/

## TL;DR

1. YouTube is the 2nd biggest Social Media source used by AI. YouTube & Reddit make up 78.2% of AI citations on Social Media.
2. Perplexity leads at 38.7% of YouTube citations, followed by Google AI Overviews (36.6%); Gemini and Copilot rarely cite YouTube.
3. 94% of AI citations go to long-form YouTube videos, not Shorts.
4. Timestamped citations appear exclusively within Google platforms — 73% in AI Overviews, 27% in AI Mode — with zero instances in ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or Perplexity.
5. 78% of timestamped videos are cited repeatedly, often across 2-5 chapters, effectively multiplying citation surface area from a single asset.

## Social Media Citation Rankings

Across 5.54% of total AI citations that come from social platforms:

1. Reddit: 46.4%
2. YouTube: 31.8%
3. LinkedIn: 13%
4. Facebook: 5%
5. Instagram: 2.2%
6. Others: <2%

| Gemini | 0.2% | Limited platform diversity |

## Content Format Analysis

- Long-form videos: 94% of citations
- YouTube Shorts: 5.7% of citations
- Other formats (playlists, livestreams): 0.3%

## Video Structure Insights

- Only 31% of cited videos contained timestamp signals.
- 78% of timestamped videos received multiple citations across different chapters.
- Timestamped citations appear only within Google AI Overviews (73%) and Google AI Mode (27%) — zero in ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or Perplexity.
- Video duration distribution: 5-10 min = 26.1% of citations; 10-20 min = 32.1% (largest cluster); >20 min = 17.6%. Median cited-video duration is under 8 minutes.

## Correlation Analysis (Pearson's r)

Does NOT predict citation frequency: view count (-0.03), likes (-0.02), subscriber count (-0.03), channel total views (-0.03), video duration (0.02).

Weakly correlates: description length (0.31), description hashtags (0.20), recency (~0.3).

## Methodology

100+ million AI citation instances over 30 days, across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, and Gemini. Pearson correlation coefficient used to test linear relationships between video metadata and citation frequency. Scope limitation: the study examined already-cited videos — strongest for explaining repeated-citation behavior, not initial eligibility for being cited at all.

## Main Takeaways (source's own framing)

1. Structure matters more than popularity — "AI citation behavior looks less like recommendation and more like reference selection."
2. Timestamps are a Google-specific advantage, enabling segment-level citations that function like subheadings in text documents.
3. Long-form reference content (explainers, tutorials, walkthroughs, case studies) dominates over short-form reach content.
4. Platform divergence is significant — a YouTube strategy optimized for Perplexity won't necessarily work for ChatGPT or Gemini.
5. Channel size is irrelevant — small channels get cited as often as large ones based on answer quality/clarity.
6. Treat descriptions as machine-readable metadata: summaries, entities, and properly formatted chapters.
