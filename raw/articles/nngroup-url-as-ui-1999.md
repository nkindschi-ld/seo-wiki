## Six essential requirements for usable URLs

1. **Domain names** should be easy to remember and spell.
2. **Short URLs** are preferable.
3. **Easy-to-type** construction matters.
4. **Structure visualization** through URL hierarchy.
5. **"Hackable" URLs** — users can navigate up the information architecture by
   removing URL segments.
6. **Persistent URLs** that remain stable over time.

## Specific URL best practices

- Keep URLs **under 78 characters** to prevent line wrapping in email.
- Employ **all-lowercase** formatting since users cannot reliably recall
  capitalization. "People can't remember the difference between upper-case and
  lower-case characters."
- Implement **spelling-checking webservers** to handle inevitable typos.
- **Never move pages**; maintain permanent URLs to prevent linkrot.
- Implement **redirect mechanisms** for temporary URLs pointing to permanent
  content locations.

## Domain name strategy

- For English-language, worldwide sites: pursue **.com domains**.
- For other contexts: use country-specific domains (.is, etc.) to accurately
  represent local appeal and service focus.

## Notable predictions & updates

- Nielsen predicted domain names would decline in importance within 3–5 years,
  but acknowledged in 2005 that direct URL entry remained relevant.
- A 2007 Microsoft Research eye-tracking study found users spend **24% of their
  gaze time looking at the URLs** in search results, particularly when assessing
  destination credibility.
