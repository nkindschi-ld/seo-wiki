# The Shortlist is the New Shelf

**Authors:** Kevin Indig (Analysis), Eric Van Buskirk (Research, Clickstream Solutions), Jasman Singh (Lead AI Analyst)
**Publication:** Profound Blog (Data & Research)
**Published:** 2026-07-14
**URL:** https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/the-shortlist-is-the-new-shelf

---

## Summary (extracted via WebFetch, 2026-07-22)

### Core finding

ChatGPT visibility strongly predicts purchase decisions. Chosen brands
appeared roughly twice as often in ChatGPT's answers (24% share of
voice) as rejected options (11%), correlation **0.57** between
visibility and being chosen.

### Category-specific correlations (Appendix A)

| Category | Correlation | Brands / picks |
|---|---|---|
| Grocery | 0.97 | 5 brands, 17 picks |
| Pet insurance | 0.87 | 10 brands, 54 picks |
| Budgeting | 0.76 | 7 brands, 45 picks |
| Nutrition apps | 0.57 | 7 brands, 25 picks |
| Travel insurance | 0.26 | 6 brands, 19 picks |
| Meal delivery | 0.13 | 10 brands, 34 picks |
| Fitness trackers | 0.00 | 3 brands, 7 picks |
| Fitness apps | -0.44 | 5 brands, 12 picks |
| Coaching | -0.98 | 6 brands, 16 picks |

Thin categories (under 5 brands) flagged by the authors as directional
only, not reliable.

### User behavior (no-click / trust)

- 92.8% of tasks ended with no meaningful click to the open web
- 48.9% accepted ChatGPT's recommendation with no follow-up
- 38.8% asked ChatGPT to justify its recommendation in-chat (not a web
  click)
- Only 3.2% verified information on the open web
- 65.4% of tasks involved scanning rather than close reading
- Mean task duration: 236 seconds
- 81.9% of participants reported increased confidence in their
  decision
- 91.1% rated ChatGPT's information 4-5/5 for reliability

### Attention / comparison grids

- Product/brand comparison grids held attention longest: 35.9% of
  tasks
- Feature-comparison grids: 15.0% of tasks
- Participant quotes: "These tables are epic"; "This table format
  makes it easier to scan through the differences quickly"; "I love
  that it's telling me right off the biggest downside of each"

### Task outcomes (Appendix B)

- Decision-ready: 57.1% (95% CI 50.5-63.5%)
- Comparing options: 36.5% (95% CI 30.4-43.1%)
- Oriented, not comparing: 5.5%
- Exited early: 0.9% (n=2)
- Overwhelmed: 0.0%
- 93.6% of tasks were comparing or decision-ready; 88% involved
  low-to-medium category knowledge

### Unknown brands can win

Participants expressed genuine interest in and willingness to consider
brands they had never heard of (e.g. "I've actually never heard of
Misfits Market, and I am super interested") when ChatGPT presented them
with clear "best for X" positioning — familiarity was not a
prerequisite for consideration.

### Four framing levers identified

1. Raise share of voice in low-prior-knowledge categories
2. Earn a grid row with clean specs and a "best for X" label
3. Maintain current, accurate pricing across all model sources
4. Address and disclose downsides plainly rather than omitting them

### Framing / metaphors used by the authors

"The shortlist is the new shelf." "Shelf space predicts the sale."
"Inside ChatGPT, share of voice is shelf space." "Getting lifted into
that table is the AEO equivalent of owning a featured snippet." "Your
row is your shelf placement."

### Methodology

- **Sample**: 56 participants, 224 tasks completed, 221 codable tasks,
  51 usable think-aloud transcripts
- **Data**: 6,882 ChatGPT citations across 36 representative queries
- **Participant profile**: 66% used AI chatbots daily, 93% weekly+
  (skews toward experienced AI users)
- **Methods**: desktop video screen recording + think-aloud protocol,
  AI-assisted transcripts cross-checked against video, pre/during/
  post-task questionnaires, share-of-voice measured via Profound's SoV
  feature, 95% confidence intervals (Wilson score method)
- **Study platform**: UX Tweak
- **Analysis team**: Kevin Indig, Eric Van Buskirk (Clickstream
  Solutions), Jasman Singh, plus a PhD-level analyst with experimental-
  modeling experience

### Limitations (stated by the authors)

Session-level analysis (each task treated independently, not a
longitudinal user journey); think-aloud protocol doesn't perfectly
mirror everyday browsing behavior; correlations in thin categories
(<5 brands) are directional only; sample skews toward experienced AI
users (66% daily), so findings may not generalize to infrequent AI
users.
