## Core definitions

**AI keyword research**: "the practice of mapping the prompts, sub-questions, and entities that AI engines use to assemble answers, so your page becomes a cited source rather than just a ranked result."

GEO positioned as distinct from SEO — success measured via citations/answer inclusion, not rankings/clicks.

## Seven keyword categories

1. Primary prompts (direct questions)
2. Conversational variants (natural rewording)
3. Sub-questions (likely follow-ups)
4. Entity terms (products, brands, standards)
5. Comparison queries ("X vs Y," "best tools")
6. Task-based phrases ("how to," templates, checklists)
7. Trust signals (pricing, case studies, methodology)

## Recommended content structure

Page-level: direct citable answer in first 100 words, "Key Takeaways" block, H2s as sub-questions, short paragraphs, lists/tables/examples, consistent entity references, original data, Article + FAQPage schema.

Passage-level: sentences must function independently of surrounding context for extraction.

## Measurement framework (six metrics)

Prompt coverage, citation frequency, answer inclusion/unlinked mentions, entity association, assisted traffic/conversions, share of citations.

## Common mistakes

Targeting only high-volume head terms, ignoring follow-ups, generic non-unique content, skipping entities/comparisons/definitions, unstructured text blocks, measuring rankings instead of citations, over-investing in unproven tactics.

## Recommended workflow (six steps)

Capture seed prompts, mine real prompt language (Search Console, Reddit, support tickets, AI autocompletes), generate sub-questions, build entity map, audit already-cited pages, convert findings into retrieval-friendly content blocks.
