## Studies

### 1. Does the Citation Matter? Quality, Grounding, and Physician-Rubric Accuracy of Google's AI Overviews for Medical Information — Northwestern University (Sam Agnoli … Emőke-Ágnes Horvát), 2026-10-29
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39339 · **Type:** cross-sectional audit (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed) · **Rigor:** 5/5
**Method:** 3,610 HealthBench health questions sent to Google via SerpAPI (US English), 2026-09-16 to 09-18; AIO text, citations and top-5 organic captured; source quality, statement grounding and physician-rubric accuracy scored with validated classifiers (kappa .61–.68); code and data released
**Key findings:** • AIOs triggered on 96.4% of health questions, citing 8.5 sources on average • Only 47.1% of scorable cited statements were supported by their citation (56.7% after classifier correction), and 20.8% were uncited factual claims • AIOs earned 38.2% of physician-rubric points, and grounding was unrelated to accuracy (ρ = .01)
**Conflicts of interest:** none apparent
**Wiki fit:** possible conflict with [[ai-overview-grounding-and-fidelity]] on the grounded share (wiki holds ~89% grounded; this finds ~47–57% in health); agrees that fidelity is independent of accuracy

### 2. Do Coding Agents Follow AGENTS.md? The Effect of Policy Wording on Instruction Compliance — Nara Institute of Science and Technology (Rikuto Tsuchida … Kenichi Matsumoto), 2026-11-03
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04367 · **Type:** controlled experiment (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed) · **Rigor:** 5/5
**Method:** one policy appended to real agent context files in four wording styles; 86 CTXbench tasks from 10 repos, 3 runs each, on Claude Code 2.1.227 (claude-opus-5, claude-sonnet-5) and qwen-code 0.21.2 (gpt-oss-120b, Qwen3-Coder-Next); Friedman and Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests; replication package
**Key findings:** • claude-sonnet-5 compliance ranged from 71.6% (prescriptive) to 17.3% (prohibitive) • Conditional "if X then Y" wording was followed significantly less often than prescriptive or explanatory wording • Open models on qwen-code barely complied (gpt-oss-120b peaked at 20.4%)
**Conflicts of interest:** none apparent (JSPS-funded)
**Wiki fit:** extends [[agents-md-instruction-files]] and [[writing-effective-agents-md-files]] (first controlled evidence on how wording changes compliance)

### 3. AI Overviews Currently Appear for 83% of Branded Searches — Ahrefs (Ryan Law, Xibeijia Guan), 2026-10-02
**Link:** https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/ · **Type:** SERP crawl analysis plus weekly brand panel · **Rigor:** 4/5
**Method:** 232.8M US desktop SERP crawls (92.5M branded, 140.2M non-branded), July–September 2026; 100 Interbrand brand names checked at 14 weekly checkpoints; citation analysis of 57–58 AIOs with visible sources
**Key findings:** • AIOs appeared on 82.9% of branded-keyword results on 2026-09-29; the monthly branded rate rose from 61.3% to 73.3% • The branded vs non-branded gap narrowed from 12.2 to 5 points • Wikipedia was cited in 71.9% of brand-panel AIOs, YouTube 38.6%, LinkedIn 22.8%
**Conflicts of interest:** Ahrefs sells the tools used (Site Explorer, Brand Radar)
**Wiki fit:** extends [[aio-ctr-impact]] (AIO prevalence on branded queries) and [[brand-entity-seo-strategy]]

### 4. Understanding ChatGPT Citations: Evidence from Production Web Search Across Model Generations — Xi'an Jiaotong University / NUS (Yunxuan Jiang … Xiaokui Xiao), 2026-10-11
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15382 · **Type:** observational measurement study (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed) · **Rigor:** 4/5
**Method:** SSE traces from ChatGPT Web for 80 Search Arena queries (English and Chinese) across four model settings, 2026-03-29 to 07-29; 4,238 runs compared with Google (SerpAPI) and the Responses API; permutation tests with Holm correction, bootstrap CIs, XGBoost predictor
**Key findings:** • Fan-out varies sharply by model: 11.82 executed queries per run for 5.4-thinking vs 1.06 for 5.3-instant • Repeat-run URL Jaccard was only ~0.30, and 7.7–9.4% of candidates formed a stable core • Google plus Responses API recover only 26–28% of cited URLs but ~50% of cited hostnames, and API citation precision against ChatGPT Web was just 16–23%
**Conflicts of interest:** none apparent
**Wiki fit:** extends [[query-fanout]] (fan-out count depends on model setting, relevant to the open 2.1 vs 7.61 conflict) and [[ai-visibility-measurement-methodology]] (API proxies don't match the consumer product)

### 5. When Instruction Files Go Stale: Documentation Debt and Hidden Cost in Coding Agents — North Carolina State University (Tural Mehtiyev … Wesley Assunção), 2026-11-06
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09802 · **Type:** controlled experiment on real repositories (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed) · **Rigor:** 4/5
**Method:** three real stale lines from root AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md files; 22 seeded-bug tasks under stale, corrected and removed versions with Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 in Claude Code 2.1.316; 732 trajectories analyzed at the tool-call level; replication package
**Key findings:** • Agents followed a stale test command in 78 of 80 runs, but task success barely changed while tool calls rose (7.8 vs 5.8) • In 19 of 40 Sonnet stale runs, the first verification ran no tests while reporting success • Only 21 of 133 stale-path encounters were disclosed, and no run proposed fixing the instruction file
**Conflicts of interest:** none apparent
**Wiki fit:** extends [[agents-md-instruction-files]] (quantified cost of stale instructions)

### 6. Toward an Evidence-Based Template for Agent Instruction Files — University of Windsor (Mehedi Hasan Shanto … Alioune Ngom), 2026-11-02
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02383 · **Type:** repository mining study plus exploratory generation evaluation (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed) · **Rigor:** 4/5
**Method:** 1,000 repos from the AIDev dataset (500 above, 500 below median stars), files accessed October 2026; 3,387 instruction files coded into 16 categories (kappa 0.80); 5,396 heading-level edit events; replication package
**Key findings:** • 66.2% of high-popularity repos have an agent instruction file vs 50.2% of low-popularity repos • AGENTS.md + CLAUDE.md is the most common multi-format pair, with 16.1% of paired sections diverging and none contradicting • 40.4% of files have only one recorded modification
**Conflicts of interest:** none apparent
**Wiki fit:** extends [[agents-md-instruction-files]] (adoption and maintenance data)

### 7. "If I Had to Buy Just ONE": Auditing AI-Generated Product Recommendations — Maastricht University Law & Tech Lab (Lucas Uberti-Bona Marin, Thales Bertaglia et al.), 2026-09-16
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18729 · **Type:** algorithm audit (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed) · **Rigor:** 4/5
**Method:** 117 commercial-advice queries run three times under five conditions (ChatGPT and Gemini interfaces and APIs, Google AIO), early September 2026, via Dutch residential proxies; 1,536 responses; LLM-assisted annotation validated against human gold standards
**Key findings:** • ChatGPT voiced first-person personal preference in 79% of product answers vs 7% for Gemini • Product Jaccard across repeat runs was only 0.18 (ChatGPT) to 0.42 (AIO) • Interface vs API displayed-domain overlap was just 12–15%
**Conflicts of interest:** none apparent
**Wiki fit:** extends [[ai-mediated-commercial-persuasion]]; extends [[ai-visibility-measurement-methodology]] (API-based tracking diverges from what consumers see)

### 8. Breaking News Out of the Filter Bubble: Generative AI Search Diversifies Collective Attention — University of Texas at Dallas (Heeseung Andrew Lee, Dokyun Lee et al.), 2026-09-30
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38946 · **Type:** randomized field experiment (working paper, not yet peer-reviewed) · **Rigor:** 4/5
**Method:** A/B test of AI answers on Washington Post site search, 2024-10-01 to 11-07, 37,561 readers assigned by cookie; BERTopic over 370,654 articles; bootstrap CIs and permutation tests; data under a DUA
**Key findings:** • AI answers raised shared information consumption per reader by 69% and total consumption by 82% • Clicks on conventional results fell 20.8 points while clicks on cited sources rose 14.3 points • Opens per search fell 18%, but readers searched 29% more, so per-reader article consumption rose 6%
**Conflicts of interest:** none apparent (Washington Post supplied data; no author employed there)
**Wiki fit:** extends [[aio-ctr-impact]] (randomized evidence that cited sources gain clicks as conventional results lose them, on a publisher's own search)

### 9. Do GEO Gains Survive the Live Web? LIVE-GEO — University of Michigan / UIUC (Runze Kong … Haohan Wang), 2026-10-31
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01126 · **Type:** live-web field experiment (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed) · **Rigor:** 3/5
**Method:** webpages for 80 fictional brands, each with an original and five GEO rewrites, tested on live ChatGPT (GPT-6), Gemini 3.6 Thinking and Grok plus five direct-text LLMs, varying order, price and identity masking; collection period not stated, so capped at 3
**Key findings:** • Live engines agreed on the top product for only 49–66% of brands, so GEO gains don't generalize across engines • Candidate order alone moved one method's ChatGPT Top-1 rate from 73.8% to 93.8% • A numerical verifier flagged every "Statistics Addition" rewrite; demoting flagged items dropped it to 0% Top-1
**Conflicts of interest:** run with an unnamed startup partner; the leading optimizer used live ChatGPT feedback
**Wiki fit:** possible conflict with [[generative-engine-optimization]] on statistics addition as a GEO lever; extends the offline-benchmark caveats there

### 10. What Content Do AI Engines Cite? 617,985 Citations Across 7 Engines and 5 Industries — OtterlyAI (Thaylise Nakamoto), 2026-10-14
**Link:** https://otterly.ai/blog/content-types-cited-by-ai-study/ · **Type:** citation content-type analysis · **Rigor:** 3/5
**Method:** five US brand reports sending 100 buyer prompts a day to 7 engines, September 2026; 617,985 citations across 85,330 URLs classified by format (84% coverage) and publisher type (73%); prompt set weighted toward "best/top" phrasing
**Key findings:** • Listicles were 66.6% of classified citations (about 55% for open-ended prompts) • Brand-owned pages took 38.9% of publisher-identified citations, and brands published 35.6% of cited listicles • 44.8% of listicle/comparison/guide citations went to publishers disclosing affiliate commissions
**Conflicts of interest:** Otterly sells GEO monitoring and discloses a partnership with a paid-publisher marketplace (Getfluence)
**Wiki fit:** agrees with [[listicles-in-ai-search]]; extends it with affiliate-publisher share

### 11. Do AI Engines Read Your Open Graph Tags? We Hid Codes in Ours to Find Out — OtterlyAI (Thaylise Nakamoto), 2026-10-22
**Link:** https://otterly.ai/blog/do-ai-engines-read-open-graph-tags/ · **Type:** controlled canary-token experiment · **Rigor:** 3/5
**Method:** unique codes in og:title, og:description and body text of three posts plus a control; 25 prompts daily on 7 engines, 2026-10-06 to 10-18; 2,275 logged-out answers plus 100 logged-in manual tests
**Key findings:** • 0 of 504 logged-out questions asking for OG content returned the correct code • Engines often passed off the body-text code as the OG value (146 cases across 5 engines) • Only logged-in Claude read OG tags correctly (12 of 12); body text was read reliably
**Conflicts of interest:** Otterly sells GEO monitoring; tested on its own blog
**Wiki fit:** extends [[open-graph-protocol]]: first measured evidence for the currently unevidenced "no AI-search effect" claim

### 12. How does personal context change LLM responses? — Profound (Allison Huang), 2026-10-29
**Link:** https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/how-does-personal-context-change-llm-responses · **Type:** controlled personalization experiment · **Rigor:** 3/5
**Method:** 7 synthetic memory profiles plus a control on ChatGPT Instant and Muse Spark with web search, 14 shopping prompts × 10 runs, 2026-10-19; 2,240 answers; Jaccard on named entities and embedding distance
**Key findings:** • Repeat answers for the same user overlapped ~42% (ChatGPT) and ~18% (Muse Spark), about double the overlap with no-context users • 19% of ChatGPT's and 54% of Muse Spark's search queries contained details from the user's context • Relevant memory was referenced in 83–88% of answers
**Conflicts of interest:** Profound sells AI-visibility tracking; personalization argues for persona-based tracking
**Wiki fit:** extends [[personal-context-signals-in-ai-search]] (memory as well as Gmail/Photos)

### 15. Three AI models picked different products from the same Shopify catalog — No Hacks (Slobodan Manic), 2026-10-24
**Link:** https://nohacks.co/blog/three-ai-models-picked-different-products · **Type:** controlled agent product-selection experiment · **Rigor:** 3/5
**Method:** top-20 Shop MCP results saved for each search (2026-10-15 and 10-24), given to Claude Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 5 and Opus 5 with a bare shopping prompt; 1,980 picks including position-reversal and constraint tests; reasons coded by keyword; Anthropic models only
**Key findings:** • The three models' most-picked product matched in 0 of 6 searches, though each model was internally consistent (67–94%) • Moving the top product from first to last cut its picks to near zero, yet none of 480 stated reasons mentioned position • 61 of 63 "clinically proven" reasons were copied from the listing without verification
**Conflicts of interest:** the author runs a machine-first site-optimization consultancy
**Wiki fit:** extends [[agentic-web-optimization]] (how shopping agents choose from catalog data)
